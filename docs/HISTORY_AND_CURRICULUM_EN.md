# History of educational software and meaning of each subject's syllabus (English)

## Part A — A brief history of educational software

The history of educational software is the story of how technology took over roles that once belonged only to the teacher: **presenting content, assessing, simulating and accompanying**.

| Era | Milestone | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1943 | Analogue flight simulators (type 19 synthetic radar trainer) | First use of simulation as a safe substitute for real practice |
| 1960 | **PLATO** (Programmed Logic for Automatic Teaching Operations), University of Illinois | First generalised computer-assisted instruction system; assessment and feedback adapted to alternative answers |
| 1963 | IBM + Stanford University (IMSSS, Patrick Suppes) | First large-scale CAI curriculum, implemented in schools in California and Mississippi |
| 1967 | **LOGO** (Feurzeig, Papert, Solomon) | A language designed for students: learning to program fosters logical thinking (the turtle) |
| 1969 | **TICCIT** | Minicomputer-and-television instruction system aimed at higher education and communication |
| 1972 | **PLATO IV** | Bitmap graphics, touchscreen, audio and forums: it invented much of the modern educational interface |
| 1975–1983 | Altair 8800, Apple II, Commodore PET/VIC-20/64 | Personal computers became affordable; educational software reached homes and classrooms |
| 1980s | MECC, Broderbund, The Learning Company; *The Oregon Trail*, *Number Munchers* | "Edutainment" turned repetitive practice into a game with story and reward |
| 1990s | Multimedia CD-ROM encyclopedias; Wikipedia (2001) as collaborative reference | Reference stopped being only printed text and later became collectively edited |
| 2000 | **GCompris** (Bruno Coudoin) | Native free educational software for Linux; today 190+ activities on every platform |
| 2002 | **Moodle** and **PhET** (Carl Wieman) | Open-source learning management and free interactive science simulations |
| 2007 | **Scratch** (MIT Media Lab) and **Kiwix** | Block-based programming for children; offline Wikipedia for offline contexts |
| 2008 | **Khan Academy** | Free online resources and large-scale adaptive practice |
| 2012 onward | MOOCs, Duolingo, Hour of Code, micro:bit | Ubiquitous, gamified learning with digital competence as a core axis |
| 2020 onward | Remote teaching and LLM assistants | Massive distance learning; automated AI assessment appears, along with its limits (inconsistent feedback, opaque scoring) |

**Historical lesson:** tools change, principles remain: immediate feedback, scaffolding, spaced practice and the chance to create. The CEELTES framework (Karolčík et al., 2015) evaluates educational software in four areas: technical/user attributes, content and operation, educational use and learning, and psychological-pedagogical dimension.

Sources: [Educational software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_software), [PLATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLATO_(computer_system)), [GCompris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GCompris), [Logo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logo_(programming_language)), [Scratch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scratch_(programming_language)), [PhET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhET_Interactive_Simulations), [Moodle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moodle), [Kiwix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiwix), [Khan Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Academy), [CEELTES](https://doi.org/10.15388/infedu.2015.14).

## Part B — Meaning of each subject's syllabus

A "syllabus" is not merely a list of topics: it is a **sequence of concepts with an educational purpose**. Below: what is taught, why it matters, and which catalog tool makes it concrete.

### Mathematics
- **Typical syllabus:** number and operations → fractions → plane geometry → measurement → functions and graphs → elementary statistics → problem solving.
- **Meaning:** trains logical thinking, abstraction and modelling; geometry connects tangible space with symbols; functions introduce dependence between variables.
- **In the catalog:** GCompris (counting), GeoGebra (geometry/algebra), KAlgebra (functions), Kig (constructions), Step (physical models with numbers).
- **Example:** draw a parabola in GeoGebra, move the parameter and explain how the table changes.

### Language / reading and writing
- **Syllabus:** phonological awareness → fluent reading → spelling → vocabulary → grammar → text composition → oral expression.
- **Meaning:** automated reading frees working memory for comprehension; spelling and vocabulary are tools of precision; composition turns ideas into structured discourse.
- **In the catalog:** Tux Typing and KTouch (keyboard), KAnagram and KWordQuiz (vocabulary), KHangMan (spelling), LibreOffice and Scribus (production), Anki (review).
- **Example:** create 20 vocabulary cards in Anki and write a paragraph in LibreOffice using ten of them.

### Science (physics, chemistry, biology, astronomy)
- **Syllabus:** observation and measurement → states of matter → forces and energy → systems and models → human body and ecosystems → sky and universe → scientific method.
- **Meaning:** not accumulating facts, but learning to formulate hypotheses, design experiments, interpret evidence and change one's mind when data demands it.
- **In the catalog:** Step (forces and energy), KStars (astronomy), Kiwix (offline reference), PhET (simulations, web link), Xournal++ (lab notebook).
- **Example:** simulate an inclined plane in Step, predict the travel distance and compare with the simulation.

### Technology and programming (digital competence)
- **Syllabus:** digital literacy → file management → logic and algorithms → block programming → textual programming → debugging → security and privacy → digital citizenship.
- **Meaning:** moving from consuming technology to creating it; debugging teaches that errors are information; privacy is part of the curriculum, not an appendix.
- **In the catalog:** GCompris (mouse/keyboard), Scratch (blocks), Thonny (Python with step visualisation), Freeplane (planning).
- **Example:** program a quiz game in Scratch with a score variable and a restart.

### History and geography (social sciences)
- **Syllabus:** time and chronology → historical sources → societies and change → maps and scale → location and relief → climate and settlement → citizenship and heritage.
- **Meaning:** building identity and memory, understanding the present as the result of processes, and reading space as a system of relations rather than a list of names.
- **In the catalog:** KGeography (countries and capitals), Marble (3D globe), Kiwix (offline Wikipedia), LibreOffice (timelines).
- **Example:** locate a country in Marble and explain how its relief conditions economic activity.

### Art and visual expression
- **Syllabus:** elements and principles of design → colour and composition → drawing and perspective → digital image and editing → vector design → editorial production.
- **Meaning:** developing a critical eye and non-verbal communication; distinguishing tool from intention; digital versions allow iteration without material cost.
- **In the catalog:** Tux Paint (introduction), GIMP (retouching), Inkscape (vector), Krita (painting), Scribus (layout).
- **Example:** create a poster in Inkscape and export it to PDF with Scribus.

### Music
- **Syllabus:** rhythm and pulse → pitch and intervals → score reading → performance → composition → sound recording and editing → music history.
- **Meaning:** coordinates motor skills, hearing and symbolisation; notation is a language read and written; recording oneself makes errors audible.
- **In the catalog:** MuseScore (scores), Audacity (recording and editing), GCompris (music activities).
- **Example:** record two versions of a melody in Audacity and compare tempo and articulation.

### English / foreign languages
- **Syllabus:** basic vocabulary → pronunciation and listening → grammar structures → graded reading → written expression → oral interaction → autonomous study.
- **Meaning:** expanding communicative competence and access to sources; spaced repetition is especially effective for vocabulary.
- **In the catalog:** Anki (cards with audio), KWordQuiz and KAnagram (drills), Kiwix (reading in English), LibreOffice (writing).
- **Example:** prepare a 30-word Anki deck and record yourself reading a paragraph in Audacity.

### Autonomy and study skills
- **Syllabus:** time management → note taking → concept maps → searching and contrasting sources → project planning → self-assessment.
- **Meaning:** learning to learn; the transversal subject that makes all others transferable.
- **In the catalog:** Freeplane (maps), Xournal++ (notes and PDFs), Kiwix (offline sources), Anki (review).
- **Example:** build a topic map in Freeplane and turn its branches into Anki cards.
